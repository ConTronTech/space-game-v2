OLD GAME REFERENCE (read-only, do not edit): /home/contolis/Documents/Space-Game-V2/space-game/space-game/include/world/starsystem.h, function `drawAtmosphere` (~line 328-373) and its call site (~line 440). It drew 3 nested transparent `gluSphere` shells slightly larger than the planet, with a single FLAT alpha per shell based only on camera DISTANCE (closer = more opaque, a coarse "inside vs outside" split), and when the camera was inside the shell it disabled depth testing entirely and applied a screen-wide tint. This is why it looked iffy: no variation across the visible face of the sphere at all (uniform alpha everywhere, no brighter glow at the grazing horizon / thin overhead look real atmospheres have), banding between the 3 discrete shells, and the "disable depth test entirely" trick for the inside case risks visual glitches (things behind other geometry bleeding through).

DESIGN: build something genuinely better, not a copy. Real atmospheric scattering makes a planet's rim glow brighter at grazing angles (limb/horizon) and thinner when looking straight down at the surface (a Fresnel-like falloff) - this is the standard cheap way to fake real atmospheric scattering WITHOUT shaders (this project is OpenGL 2.1 fixed-function only, docs/VISION.md rule 1 - no GLSL, no per-pixel shading). Achieve the falloff with PER-VERTEX alpha on a sphere shell slightly larger than the planet: for each vertex, compute the angle between its surface normal and the direction toward the camera (dot product); grazing/edge-on vertices (normal nearly perpendicular to the view direction, i.e. dot near 0) get high alpha (bright rim), vertices facing the camera nearly head-on (dot near 1) get low/near-zero alpha (clear overhead). This must be recomputed as the camera moves (the visible "rim" of a sphere shifts as you orbit it), so it is a genuine per-frame, per-vertex CPU computation (client vertex arrays, no shaders needed) - budget it carefully for the target laptop (docs/PERFORMANCE.md: GPU fill-rate bound, 2 weak CPU cores) using a LOW vertex count shell (tens to a couple hundred vertices is plenty for a soft glow - this does not need to be smooth up close like the planet surface mesh, it is background dressing), and gate it behind the quality preset (Low: either off entirely or the simplest possible single flat-alpha shell as a cheap fallback close to the old game's approach; Medium/High/Ultra: the real Fresnel-rim version, more segments at higher presets).
