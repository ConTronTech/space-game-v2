DESIGN:
1. ORBIT GUIDE: for the body whose surface is nearest (same choice as the lock, within orbit.guide_range = 6 body radii above the surface, tunable), compute the circular orbit the ship WOULD get at its current altitude: plane through the body centre containing the ship position and its velocity RELATIVE TO THE BODY (as orbit_lock does today; if the relative speed is ~0 use the ship's up/forward plane), circular speed v = sqrt(mu/r) with the existing mu model (orbit.gravity_scale x radius^2). Draw it as a thin curved line: a circle (GL_LINE_STRIP, 96 segments, camera-relative double->float, ONE draw call, additive, depth test on / write off, fades out with distance so it is calm at far range) centred on the body, with a brighter arc segment AHEAD of the ship (the next ~90 degrees along the direction of travel: this is the "curved vector" showing where the orbit will take you) and small tick marks every 30 degrees; plus a short velocity marker at the ship (the direction it would move on the orbit) so the player can compare it with the current velocity vector (a short line along the actual relative velocity). Colours: amber = misaligned, green = within tolerance (the ring turns solid green, the ahead-arc bright green) so alignment is legible at a glance. The guide is drawn only in the range and hidden while orbit-locked/docked/warping/dead, and can be switched off (orbit.show_guide default true; quality preset row: Low draws 48 segments and no ticks).
2. ALIGNMENT IS REQUIRED: pressing O only locks when the ship is aligned: (a) the relative speed within orbit.align_speed_tol (default 12 percent) of the circular speed, (b) the angle between the relative velocity and the orbit TANGENT in the guide plane within orbit.align_angle_tol (default 10 degrees) i.e. flying roughly tangentially (NOT radially in/out), (c) radial velocity small (covered by b), (d) altitude within the engage band as today. When not aligned O REFUSES with a specific reason logged AND shown on the HUD: "speed +23 m/s too fast (need 84)", "too slow", "heading 34 degrees off the orbit (fly tangent)", "too close/too far". orbit.require_alignment default TRUE (false = the old snap behaviour, kept for testing). On lock, the ship is corrected smoothly from its current (within tolerance) state to the exact circular orbit over orbit.settle_seconds (default 1.5 s) instead of snapping (the existing analytic hold takes over after the settle).
3. HUD: a small status block near the top centre (only when a guide is active): "ORBIT PLANET 1  alt 1.0K  need 84 m/s" and a second line "SPEED +12  HEADING 4 deg  [ALIGNED - press O]" coloured amber/green (use the ORBIT LOCKED banner style; a few small quads, cached text, no per-frame allocation). Expose the guide state through a small service ship::IOrbitGuide { active(); bodyName(); altitude(); neededSpeed(); speedError(); headingErrorDeg(); aligned(); refuseReason(); } in orbit_lock_api.h so the HUD and later the cockpit radar can show it. The cockpit radar itself is out of scope.
4. NO GRAVITY YET: the ship is not pulled by bodies; the guide shows the orbit the lock would give. Append to docs/QUESTIONS.md the open question: "Should planets/moons/the sun exert real gravity on the ship (Newtonian orbits you must actually fly, the lock becoming an assist)? This would change flight a lot (Space-Engineers-like planetary gravity vs pure space)". Do not implement gravity.
5. Pure logic in orbit_lock_rules.h (no GL/SDL/engine types) with unit tests (tolerances): the guide plane and circle points (point count, radius, in-plane, orthonormal basis, degenerate zero relative velocity and radial approach), alignment tests at the edges (speed and angle tolerances), refusal reasons, the settle interpolation (start within tolerance -> exact orbit, monotonic, no NaN), arc-ahead index selection, direction of travel sign (retrograde works). Update docs/ORBIT_LOCK.md (guide, tolerances, how to line up: fly parallel at the right speed), docs/HUD.md.
